The NYC Trolley Problem

NYC culture · Transit · Swipe game

New Yorkers already have strong opinions about stations, neighborhoods, transit modes, public art, and pigeons. This turns those arguments into a deliberately impossible series of binary choices.

A Tinder-style swipe game that starts in hyper-niche transit mode — stations, subway lines, ferries, buses, and service patterns — before expanding into increasingly specific NYC cultural dilemmas.

Play Game GitHub (Private)
Why I Made This

I wanted a New York version of the trolley problem that feels local enough to reward people who actually know the city.

The first mode is transit-heavy: players choose between stations, lines, buses, ferries, and other pieces of the system. Later rounds move into neighborhood and cultural matchups such as Koreatown versus Chinatown, DUMBO versus Gramercy, Keith Haring versus other New York iconography, and one giant pigeon versus all ordinary pigeons.

The interaction is intentionally simple: swipe left or right, then see how everyone else voted.

Personality Systems Research

Assessment · Prototype · Identity

Most personality tests give you one tidy label and send you on your way. I wanted to see the messier version: overlapping traits, relative strengths, and changes over time.

A hub for personality assessment experiments: a scaled Enneagram test and an attachment-style quiz with self and partner modes, with longitudinal pattern tracking planned.

Explore Personality Systems Enneagram Quiz Attachment Quiz Project Notes GitHub (Private)
Why I Made This

Most personality tests give you a label. I wanted something a little more nuanced.

This project explores measuring personality traits on a spectrum rather than forcing people into a single category. Eventually I would like users to be able to save results and see how those patterns change over time.

Colony Cat Management

Graduate-school era · Computer vision · TNR

I live near several cat colonies, encounter street cats almost everywhere I travel, and love cats enough to revisit a graduate-school model that was only right about 70% of the time.

Computer-vision identification and tracking for community cat caretakers.

Read More GitHub
Why I Made This

I live in a part of Manhattan with several cat colonies, I have traveled extensively in places with large street cat populations, and I simply love cats.

In graduate school, I tested whether computer vision could distinguish individual community cats. The model reached roughly 70% accuracy: promising enough to be interesting and unreliable enough that I would not pretend the problem was solved.

I want to revisit it as a tool for TNR volunteers tracking colony health, population changes, and cats that may need attention.
